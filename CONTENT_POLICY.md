# DMath Learning — Editorial & Copyright Content Policy

This policy applies to learning materials, examples, proofs, exercises, problem banks, olympiad content, visualizations, and reference lists published by DMath Learning.

## 1. Core principle

DMath Learning develops an independent mathematics curriculum. Books, papers, lecture notes, and other references may be used to verify mathematical concepts, terminology, notation, historical facts, and topic coverage, but no single source should function as the page-by-page blueprint of the DMath Learning curriculum.

Mathematical ideas, formulas, methods, and standard theorem statements may be taught in DMath Learning using independently written exposition. Copyrighted expression, distinctive presentation, source-specific examples, exercise wording, illustrations, tables, and other protected material must not be copied without a suitable licence or permission.

## 2. Curriculum structure

- Organize topics according to the pedagogical goals of DMath Learning.
- Do not mirror the chapter and section sequence of one textbook one-to-one.
- Do not preserve source chapter or section numbering as DMath numbering.
- Standard mathematical topic names may be used where appropriate.
- Prefer synthesis across multiple reputable references when expanding a subject.
- Existing lesson URLs may remain stable even when a curriculum unit is reorganized.

## 3. Exposition and proofs

- Write explanations in DMath Learning's own language and structure.
- Proofs should be independently written from mathematical principles.
- A standard proof strategy may be used, but avoid sentence-level paraphrase of a source.
- State hypotheses explicitly and keep notation internally consistent.
- Where several proof approaches are known, prefer the one that best serves the DMath learning sequence rather than reproducing a source's presentation.

## 4. Examples and exercises

Every published problem should fit one of these provenance categories:

- `dmath-original`: created specifically for DMath Learning.
- `user-provided-adapted`: supplied by a user and substantially rewritten or parameterized for DMath Learning.
- `public-domain`: verified to be in the public domain.
- `licensed`: used under a licence or explicit permission compatible with the intended use.
- `competition-reference`: a competition problem whose use and attribution have been reviewed before publication.

Rules:

- Do not copy textbook exercise wording merely because the solution is rewritten.
- Changing only numbers is not sufficient when the original problem expression is distinctive.
- When adapting a user-provided exercise, alter the mathematical parameters and presentation when appropriate while preserving the intended learning objective.
- Competition names, years, and problem numbers should be added only when the provenance has been verified.

## 5. Visuals

- Prefer original SVG, CSS, canvas, or generated diagrams created for DMath Learning.
- Do not copy textbook figures, photographs, scans, tables, or page layouts without an appropriate licence or permission.
- When a third-party visual is permitted, preserve required attribution and licence information.

## 6. References

References are presented as **References and Further Reading**, not as a claim that a DMath lesson reproduces a particular section of a book.

A reference may support:

- terminology;
- theorem verification;
- conventional notation;
- topic coverage;
- further study.

A reference should not determine the wording, examples, exercise bank, visual design, or complete section sequence of a DMath Learning lesson.

## 7. Commercial-readiness rule

DMath Learning content should be written so that its continued publication does not depend on an educational-use exception or on a source that expressly forbids commercial use.

If a source carries a non-commercial restriction, use it only as background for identifying mathematical topics. Do not reproduce its distinctive content, exercises, solutions, illustrations, or source-specific organization in monetized DMath Learning material.

## 8. Pre-publication checklist

Before a new lesson or problem set is merged:

- [ ] The explanation is independently written.
- [ ] Definitions and theorem statements use standard mathematical language.
- [ ] Proofs are independently structured and written.
- [ ] Examples are original, licensed, public-domain, or otherwise cleared.
- [ ] Exercise provenance is known.
- [ ] No textbook figure, scan, table, or screenshot has been copied.
- [ ] The lesson is placed according to the DMath Curriculum rather than a source TOC.
- [ ] References are listed as supporting/further-reading sources.
- [ ] Any required attribution or licence notice is present.
- [ ] The content remains suitable if DMath Learning is monetized.

## 9. Current curriculum convention

The public learning sequence is identified as **DMath Curriculum v1**. Subject hubs use DMath Learning units rather than source chapter numbering. Existing source references are retained only as references and further reading.
